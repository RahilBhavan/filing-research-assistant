Business

Harbor Manufacturing produces industrial water pumps and replacement valves. It sells through independent distributors to municipal utilities and commercial buildings.

Most equipment sales occur under fixed-price purchase orders. Revenue is recognized when control of the equipment transfers to the distributor.

Management discussion and analysis

For the year ended September 30, 2025, pump revenue grew because distributors increased orders for replacement pumps. Higher selling prices also contributed to revenue growth.

Production costs increased during 2025 because copper and steel prices rose. Higher material costs were partly offset by reduced overtime at the eastern factory.

Operating cash flow declined during 2025 because the company built inventory to protect against supplier delays.

The largest distributor represented 24 percent of annual revenue in 2025. The company depends on this distributor for access to several municipal customers.

At September 30, 2025, the company employed 620 people. Factory operations employed 480 people.

Risk factors

A single supplier provides the cast housings used in the largest pump model. An interruption at that supplier could delay shipments and increase production costs.

Reduced municipal infrastructure spending could lower demand for water pumps. Municipal budget decisions may delay purchase orders.

Product defects could lead to warranty claims and equipment recalls. The company may incur repair costs and lose customer relationships.
